What causes climate change

Heat-trapping gases have built up in the atmosphere because of human activity. The more we add, the more heat stays near the surface.

How the greenhouse effect works

Sunlight warms Earth

Sunlight passes through the atmosphere and heats land and oceans.

Earth sends heat back out

The surface radiates heat as infrared energy toward space.

Gases hold some of it in

Carbon dioxide, methane and other gases absorb that heat and re-emit it downward. More gas means more trapped heat.

Pakistan’s part in the problem

Pakistan’s emissions come mainly from energy, agriculture and livestock, and transport. Per person they are a small fraction of those in wealthy countries, yet Pakistan ranks among the most climate-vulnerable nations. That gap is why it argues for climate justice and funding for loss and damage.

What climate change is doing

2024 was the first calendar year more than 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels. Every extra fraction of a degree raises the risk of extreme events.

What can be done

Cutting emissions (mitigation) stops the problem growing. Preparing for impacts (adaptation) protects people from what is already locked in. Both are needed.

What you can do

Use less energy

Efficient appliances, LED lighting and good insulation or shading cut bills and emissions together.

Move and eat smarter

Walk, cycle or share rides where you can, and waste less food, since rotting food releases methane.

Plant, prepare and speak up

Plant native trees, know your local flood and heat warnings, and ask leaders for climate action and funding.

Climate finance matters as much as technology: poorer countries need money to adapt and to recover from losses they did little to cause.
